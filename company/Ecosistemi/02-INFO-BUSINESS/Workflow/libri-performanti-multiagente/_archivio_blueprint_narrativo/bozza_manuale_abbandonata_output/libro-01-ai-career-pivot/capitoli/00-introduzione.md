# Introduction

## Why This Book Exists

Somewhere between "I hate my job" and "I have a new job," there used to be a long, expensive, uncertain gap. You'd spend months figuring out what else you were qualified for, more months building the skills or credentials to prove it, and then months applying into a black hole of applicant tracking systems that never called back. Career changers accepted this because there was no faster way through.

That gap has started to close, and not because career change got easier in some abstract sense. It's because a specific set of tools — large language models like ChatGPT and Claude, plus the growing ecosystem built around them — turned out to be unusually good at exactly the tasks that used to make career change slow: figuring out what you actually know how to do, translating that into language a stranger in another industry will understand, researching fields you've never worked in, and producing the volume of tailored material that a real job search requires.

This book is a practical guide to using those tools for one specific job: changing your career, not just changing your employer. If you're looking for your next role in the same field you already work in, plenty of the tactics here will still help, but the book is built around the harder problem — moving from a career where you have credibility to one where you don't yet, and doing it without wasting a year figuring out how.

## What This Book Is Not

It is not a book that tells you AI will get you hired. Nothing gets you hired except you, doing the work, in front of the right person, at the right time. AI does not have judgment about which opportunities are worth pursuing, it does not have a track record employers can check, and it cannot sit in an interview for you. Every chapter in this book treats AI as exactly what it is: a extremely capable research assistant, drafting partner, and thinking tool that removes friction from tasks that used to take too long to do well. The judgment, the decisions, and the actual relationship-building stay with you.

It is also not a book about AI taking your job. That's a real conversation, and an important one, but it's a different book. This one assumes you've already decided — or are seriously considering — that you want to move toward a different career, for whatever reason: your industry is shrinking, you're burned out, you found something that interests you more, or the work you do is changing in ways you don't want to follow. The "why" is yours. This book is about the "how."

## Who This Book Is For

You'll get the most out of this book if you recognize yourself in one of these situations:

- You've been in one field for years and are considering a real pivot — not a lateral move, but a genuine change in industry or function.
- You started exploring a career change and got stuck, overwhelmed by how much research, skill-building, and repositioning it seemed to require.
- You've used ChatGPT or Claude for small tasks — writing an email, summarizing an article — but haven't used it systematically for something as complex as a full career transition.
- You're skeptical of "AI will change your life" claims and want a grounded, specific, step-by-step approach instead of hype.

If you're a student choosing a first career, some of this will apply, but the book assumes you have existing professional experience to translate — that's the harder and more common case, and it's the one AI tools help with the most, because they're good at finding connections between things you already know and things you don't.

## How the Book Is Structured

The ten chapters follow the actual sequence of a career change, in the order most people go through it:

**Chapters 1–3** are about clarity before action: understanding how AI is actually reshaping the job market (not the panicked headline version), assessing where you stand and where you might go, and mapping the skills you already have onto fields you haven't worked in yet.

**Chapters 4–6** are about building the bridge: researching new fields efficiently, closing credibility gaps with the right kind of evidence, and rewriting your resume and professional profile so a stranger can understand your story in six seconds.

**Chapters 7–9** are about the search itself: running an AI-assisted job search that doesn't drown you in noise, networking and outreach that reads as human rather than templated, and interview preparation that builds real fluency instead of a false sense of security.

**Chapter 10** covers what happens after you get an offer: evaluating it properly and surviving the first ninety days in a field where you're still earning trust.

Each chapter ends with concrete actions and, where useful, prompt templates you can copy directly into ChatGPT or Claude and adapt. The appendix collects the most useful of these prompts in one place, along with a short list of tools referenced throughout the book, so you don't have to hunt back through chapters to find them.

## A Word on Tools

This book references ChatGPT and Claude specifically because they are, at the time of writing, the two most capable and widely used general-purpose AI assistants, and because the techniques described here work almost identically on either one. Nothing in this book requires a paid subscription to get started, though a paid tier on either platform removes usage limits that can otherwise interrupt a long research or drafting session. Where a specific feature matters — such as uploading a document for analysis, or having a very long conversation that needs to remember earlier context — that will be called out. Otherwise, assume any instruction works on whichever assistant you already have open.

Tools change fast. The underlying skill this book teaches — how to think alongside an AI system to do the specific work of a career change — will outlast whichever product name is on top of the leaderboard when you're reading this. Learn the skill, not the brand.
