Descrizione (back cover):
Elena Marsh has not slept properly in fourteen months. Not since the night her sister died.

She works the quiet hours — the night shift at a care home, where the corridors are empty and nobody asks how she is. She is good at her job. Precise. Reliable. It is only her own life she can no longer account for.

Then she finds a key in her coat pocket that she does not recognize.

Then a voicemail from her sister, dated three weeks after the funeral.

Then the security cameras show her standing in a corridor she would swear she has never walked down.

The key opens a storage unit rented in Elena's name, full of her sister's things and a notebook written in Elena's own handwriting. The neighbour who has been so kind knew Nina, and lied about it. The detective who closed the case as an accident will not quite go away. And an eighty-two-year-old woman with dementia keeps repeating a sentence that nobody else could possibly know.

Everyone tells Elena she is exhausted, and confused, and that grief does this.

Everyone is right. That is the worst part.

THE QUIET HOURS is a psychological thriller about insomnia, guilt and the stories we tell ourselves to survive what we did — and what we didn't do. The twist is not a trick: every clue is on the page, in plain sight, from chapter one. You can get there before Elena does.

Most readers don't.
