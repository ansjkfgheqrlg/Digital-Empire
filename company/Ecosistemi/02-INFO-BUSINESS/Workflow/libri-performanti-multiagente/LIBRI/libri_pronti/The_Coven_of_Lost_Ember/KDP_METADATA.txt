Titolo: The Coven of Lost Ember
Autore: Maren Ashcroft
Sottotitolo: A Found Family Witch Coven Novel
Nicchia/keyword: found family witch coven story

Descrizione (back cover):
Four women. One crack in the ground. And a binding that will not close until every one of them tells the truth.

The village of Lost Ember had a coven once. It stopped having one in 1944, when four women were not there on the third Sunday and nobody went, and for eighty one years absolutely nothing happened, which everyone took for proof that nothing ever would.

Then the ground opens across the village green, and only four people can see it for what it is.

Wren Halloway, running from the coven that raised her, with something in her bag she should not have taken. Marged Pryce, who has not worked a binding in eleven years and has let this village believe the wrong story about why. Nessa Quill, seventeen, left at a church door as a baby, who has been hearing something under the well since she was nine and has been told for eight years that it is in her head. And Bel Carrow, the warden who has spent two years hunting the first of them, and who came to this valley hoping for exactly this.

The old book is clear: the crack is closed by four, joined, and never by one. Their first attempt puts a man in hospital. Their second unravels along the same seam, in daylight, with everything done right.

Because the binding does not fail for lack of skill. It fails because it can feel that all four of them are lying.

THE COVEN OF LOST EMBER is a found family story about the price of being known. There is no chosen one, no prophecy, and no villain who enjoys it. There are four women who have every reason not to trust each other, a village of four hundred people above a seal nobody has tended since the war, and a working that asks each of them for the one thing she has never said out loud.

Some of them will say it in front of the whole village.

The hardest part is not closing the crack.

It is what closing it requires them to admit.
