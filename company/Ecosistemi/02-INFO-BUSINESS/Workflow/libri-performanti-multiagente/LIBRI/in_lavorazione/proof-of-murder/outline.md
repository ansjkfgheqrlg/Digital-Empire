# Outline — Proof of Murder

## Premessa
When Maggie Doyle reopens her late mother's small town bakery, she finds the Founder's Day Committee president dead in her proofing drawer on the morning of the festival. A recipe card in his pocket, written in her dead mother's handwriting, turns Maggie from grieving daughter into the sheriff's prime suspect.

## Ambientazione
Cedar Hollow, a small town in the Vermont hills, present day. The story unfolds during the week leading up to the town's beloved annual Founder's Day Festival in early autumn.

## Personaggi
- **Maggie Doyle** (Protagonist, new owner of Cedar Hollow Bakery): vuole To save her mother's bakery and clear her name of murder. Segreto: She left a promising pastry career in Boston after being wrongly blamed for a recipe theft scandal, and fears the town will believe the worst about her just as her old colleagues did
- **Rose Doyle** (Maggie's late mother, former bakery owner and Founder's Day treasurer): vuole To expose the committee's financial fraud before the festival three years ago. Segreto: She encoded evidence of embezzlement inside her handwritten recipe cards, never suspecting it would get her killed
- **Harold Pruitt** (The victim, president of the Founder's Day Committee): vuole To confess his role in the cover up and in Rose's death before he lost his nerve. Segreto: He helped hide the embezzlement and let Rose's death go unquestioned for three years out of guilt and fear of Denise Cobb
- **Denise Cobb** (Founder's Day Committee treasurer, and the killer): vuole To protect her hidden real estate scheme and her upcoming run for mayor. Segreto: She tampered with Rose's medication three years ago and killed Harold to stop him from confessing both crimes
- **Nate Coleman** (Deputy sheriff and Maggie's high school sweetheart): vuole To solve the case honestly while protecting Maggie from a rush to judgment. Segreto: He delayed reporting a piece of evidence for a day to give Maggie time, risking his badge to do it
- **Josie Alvarez** (Maggie's best friend and bakery assistant): vuole To help Maggie's bakery succeed and prove the town wrong about her. Segreto: She has been secretly seeing Walter Finch's son, a relationship she hides so it will not complicate Maggie's investigation
- **Walter Finch** (Local real estate developer and Denise's business partner): vuole To close a land deal tied to the festival grounds before his own debts catch up with him. Segreto: He knew about the embezzlement and stayed silent for years, though he never took part in either death
- **Diane Pruitt** (Harold's widow): vuole To protect her husband's memory and understand his final weeks. Segreto: She suspected Harold of an affair, when in truth the secret meetings she noticed were with Denise about the cover up
- **Ruth Ann Sawyer** (Rival baker who wanted to buy Cedar Hollow Bakery herself): vuole To see Maggie fail so she can finally take over the bakery. Segreto: She has been spreading rumors about Maggie out of jealousy, petty but harmless compared to the real crime
- **Bill Torres** (Cedar Hollow police chief): vuole To close the case quickly and keep the festival and town council happy. Segreto: He has quietly leaned on Denise Cobb's political favor for years, and it clouds his judgment of the case

## Atti
1. Maggie Doyle, new owner of her late mother's bakery, discovers the Founder's Day Committee president dead in her proofing drawer on festival morning, and a recipe card in his pocket written in her deceased mother's hand makes Maggie the police's prime suspect.
2. Determined to clear her name, Maggie digs into her mother's old coded recipes and the festival's finances, uncovering a years old embezzlement scheme and mounting evidence that her mother's death three years earlier was no accident, until a piece of planted false evidence nearly destroys her and her business.
3. Armed with proof from a storage unit, a torn ledger, and her mother's final warning, Maggie helps expose committee treasurer Denise Cobb as the killer of both Harold and Rose during a tense confrontation at the festival's closing ceremony, finally restoring her mother's name and securing the bakery's future.

## Capitoli
01. **Dough and a Dead Man** — Maggie arrives before dawn to start the festival bread order and finds Harold Pruitt's body slumped against her bakery's open proofing drawer, ruined dough spilling over his shoulder. She calls 911, and Deputy Nate Coleman arrives first, sealing off her kitchen hours before the festival is set to open.
02. **The Card in His Pocket** — Chief Bill Torres questions Maggie in her own dining room while forensic techs bag evidence from the kitchen she just spent her savings renovating. He shows her a stained recipe card pulled from Harold's coat pocket and asks her to confirm the handwriting is her mother's, watching her face closely when she does.
03. **Persons of Interest** — News of the recipe card spreads through Cedar Hollow by breakfast, and Ruth Ann Sawyer volunteers to supply the festival's baked goods in Maggie's place. That night Maggie and Josie tape brown paper over the windows to block out gawkers, and Maggie notices one card missing from her mother's recipe box, one she cannot remember ever being pulled.
04. **Rose's Numbers** — Maggie and Josie go through the recipe box card by card and find faint pencil numbers in the margins of a dozen cards, all dated the month before Rose died. Nate stops by off duty to warn Maggie that the state police may get involved, and he lingers long enough to notice the numbers himself.
05. **The Viewing** — At Harold's wake in the Grange hall, Maggie steels herself to pay respects and ends up seated beside his widow, Diane, who is oddly candid in her grief. Diane tells her that Harold had been distracted for weeks, slipping out at night to meetings he refused to explain.
06. **Committee Business** — Maggie visits the Founder's Day Committee office to retrieve paperwork for her canceled festival contract and finds treasurer Denise Cobb unusually solicitous, while developer Walter Finch reviews land papers at the next desk. Denise snaps a ledger shut the moment Maggie's eyes drift toward it, waving off the reaction as embarrassment over unrelated committee debts.
07. **What the Autopsy Says** — Nate meets Maggie quietly behind the bakery to tell her the county coroner ruled Harold's head wound inconsistent with a simple fall, making the case an official homicide. He also mentions a technician found the faint pressed impression of a second, more recent hand on the back of the recipe card, as if someone had written on a sheet resting on top of it.
08. **Invisible Ink** — Using the heat lamp from the proofing cabinet and a soft pencil, Maggie and Josie coax the hidden impression on the recipe card into legibility and read a fragment about the Grange numbers not adding up. Chief Torres, fed rumors by Ruth Ann about Maggie's temper and motive, publicly names her a formal suspect, and Maggie decides she will find the truth herself before the town decides it for her.
09. **Open for Business, Sort Of** — With her kitchen released back to her under police condition, Maggie reopens the bakery to a town split between loyal regulars and wary strangers, while Josie mans the register and fields whispered questions. That evening the two women dig through old Founder's Day financial records at the town library and uncover a controversial rezoning vote from three years earlier tied to Walter Finch's stalled development.
10. **A Petty Rivalry** — Maggie corners Ruth Ann Sawyer about the gossip she has been spreading and gets a defensive, almost comic confession that it was jealousy over the bakery and nothing more sinister. Flustered, Ruth Ann blurts out that she saw Harold's car parked at the Grange hall very late the night before he died, a detail she had been too embarrassed to mention.
11. **The Grange Hall at Night** — Maggie convinces Nate to walk through the Grange hall with her after hours, where they find chairs still arranged for a hushed late meeting and two cold, uncleared coffee cups. In the trash bin behind the building they find a torn ledger page whose numbers match the code from Rose's recipe cards exactly.
12. **Diane's Confession** — Maggie returns to see Diane Pruitt and gently lays out what she and Nate have found, and Diane's composure finally cracks. She admits she has been too afraid to look at Harold's credit card statements and hands over a bill for a storage unit rental she never knew he had.
13. **The Storage Unit** — Maggie and Josie open the rented unit and find boxes of old Founder's Day financial records alongside a spare key to Rose's old bakery labeled in Harold's own handwriting. Tucked beneath it is a sympathy card, never sent, that reads like a confession of guilt toward a family he never dared face.
14. **A Question of Cardiology** — With Nate's help, Maggie requests her mother's old medical file and learns Rose had no history of heart trouble before the attack that supposedly killed her. That night someone breaks into the closed bakery and tears through the recipe box without touching the register, leaving Maggie certain the truth about her mother's death has just become dangerous.
15. **Downplayed** — Chief Torres dismisses the break-in as random vandalism despite the untouched cash drawer, and Nate's insistence that it is connected puts him at odds with his own boss for the first time. Sorting through the scattered cards afterward, Maggie realizes the one card with the coded numbers is the only one actually gone.
16. **What the Pharmacist Remembers** — Maggie visits the retired town pharmacist, who still keeps old records, and learns that Rose's blood pressure medication could have been altered to trigger a fatal reaction with no one the wiser. The pharmacist recalls that only a small circle of committee friends ever had reason to be alone in Rose's kitchen those last weeks, and Denise Cobb's name comes up first.
17. **Closing Ranks** — At a public Founder's Day meeting held despite the murder, Denise pushes the committee to move past these distractions and finish the festival, growing visibly rattled when a councilman raises the stalled rezoning deal. Afterward a cornered Walter Finch warns Maggie in the parking lot to stop digging before more people get hurt, a warning that sounds like fear rather than threat.
18. **The Setup** — An anonymous tip delivers Chief Torres a forged note supposedly in Rose's hand implicating Maggie in an old grudge against Harold, and Maggie is hauled in for formal questioning while Nate is reprimanded for helping her. Released without charges but publicly humiliated, Maggie watches two festival vendors cancel their standing bakery orders and wonders, for the first time since coming home, if she should simply sell the bakery and leave.
19. **Rose's Letter** — Grieving and ready to quit, Maggie is visited by her mother's old friend and former schoolteacher, who finally shares a letter Rose wrote weeks before she died, warning that D was the real danger on the committee, not Harold. The letter restores Maggie's resolve, and she and Nate agree the evidence now points squarely at Denise Cobb.
20. **Building the Case** — Wary of Chief Torres, Maggie and Nate quietly bring their evidence, the ledger fragment, the storage unit contents, and Rose's coded cards, to a state investigator willing to listen. Together they plan to flush Denise into the open during the festival's closing ceremony, where Maggie is scheduled to unveil the Founder's Day cake in front of the whole town.
21. **The Unveiling** — On the festival's final afternoon, Maggie performs her public duty at the cake ceremony while plainclothes officers watch the crowd, and Denise finds her alone backstage afterward under the guise of concern. Fishing for what Maggie truly knows, Denise lets slip a detail about exactly how Harold's body had been positioned in the proofing drawer, a detail never released to the public.
22. **Inside the Proofing Room** — Realizing her mistake, Denise turns cold and forces Maggie back into the bakery's kitchen, locking the door and admitting she killed Harold to stop his confession and, years earlier, tampered with Rose's medication to silence her. Josie notices Maggie's absence from the crowd and alerts Nate, who breaks the kitchen door open with backup seconds after Denise raises a heavy rolling pin against Maggie.
23. **What the Town Learns** — With Denise in custody, Walter Finch cooperates fully with investigators to avoid charges, laying out years of skimmed festival funds funneled into his failed development, and Chief Torres is forced to publicly admit he pursued the wrong suspect from the start. Maggie is formally cleared, and the town's shock at Denise's arrest gives way to a quiet, collective reckoning with how long the truth about Rose had gone unspoken.
24. **The Recipe Box** — Weeks later, Cedar Hollow gathers at the bakery to dedicate a small plaque to Rose Doyle and to name a new pastry after her, and Maggie finally adds her own handwritten card to the recipe box beside her mother's. As Nate leans in the doorway waiting to walk her to the festival planning meeting for next year, Maggie closes the box knowing the bakery, and her mother's name, are finally hers to keep.
